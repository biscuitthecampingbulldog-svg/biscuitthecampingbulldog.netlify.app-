Heat guide

How hot is too hot for your pet?

Below is what we go by. Flat-faced dogs, cats, rabbits and birds all overheat sooner than people expect, and a camper without power heats up fast. Cut every number below by ten degrees for bulldogs, frenchies, pugs, boxers and Persian cats.

This is the air outside, not inside your camper
A parked camper, car or trailer runs 30 to 40°F hotter than the air outside, and it gets there in under an hour. On a mild 75°F afternoon that is well past 110°F inside. Never judge what it is like in there by what it feels like out here, and never leave a pet inside without air conditioning you can confirm is running.
The temperature lookup is done by a public weather service, which needs the coordinates to answer. We do not store them, and we do not know who asked. Everything below works with no signal at all.

Signs to stop immediately
Loud raspy breathing, thick ropey drool, bright red or purple gums, wobbling, refusing to move. Get into air conditioning and call the nearest emergency vet.
Power loss is an emergency
If the trailer air conditioning quits in summer, a pet shut inside has minutes, not hours. Know where the nearest cooled building is before you settle in for the night.

Blu Technology Review: Clean Water and a Dry Floor in Our Camper

5 out of 5 paws

I am Biscuit, and water is serious business in a house on wheels. I drink it, mama cooks with it, and daddy keeps an eye on every hose and fitting under the camper. Blu Technology makes three things that keep our water clean and our floor dry. Here is what each one does and why we use it.

Biscuit shows off our Blu Technology water setup. Music by Rafael Krux, filmmusic.io (CC BY 4.0).

1. Blu Technology WaterStop

WaterStop is a leak alarm for the camper. Small sensors sit in the spots where leaks like to start, and if one of them gets wet, WaterStop shuts the water off by itself. A slow drip under a sink can ruin a floor before anybody notices. This one catches it for us, even when we are out on a hike. You can add extra sensors to cover more spots.

2. PURBLU AR3 LED UVC 4-Stage RV Water Filter & Purification System

This is our water filter. It runs the water through four stages, and one of them is a UV light that treats the water after the filters do their job. Campground water is different at every stop, so this is how mama knows the water in my bowl is clean wherever we park.

3. Portable RV Water Softener with Smart Cap

Hard water leaves crusty spots on the faucets and the shower and builds up inside the water heater. The softener takes care of that before the water ever gets into the camper. The Smart Cap lets you recharge it without unhooking any hoses, so recharge day is quick and easy.

FXW Dog Fence Review: Our Portable Fence for Campground Life

5 out of 5 paws

I am Biscuit, and I have opinions about fences. A good fence means I get to hang out outside the camper without a leash tangling around every chair leg. A bad fence means I sit inside watching the fun. The FXW dog fence is the good kind.

Biscuit holding court in her FXW fence at the campground.

If you camp with a dog, you know the problem. Campgrounds want dogs leashed or contained, and a tie-out line lasts about ten minutes before it wraps around something. We wanted a fence we could set up at each new site and take down when we roll out.

The FXW fence is a set of metal panels that pin together into whatever shape fits your site. No tools. You set the panels in position, connect them together with the stakes, and push the stakes into the ground. Mama takes about 10 minutes to set up — we use 4 sets and the step adapter.

What I like, from a bulldog’s point of view: it is sturdy enough that I cannot push it over, tall enough that I do not try to jump it, and big enough around that I can sprawl in the sun like I own the place. Dad likes that it stacks flat for travel days, and there’s a carrying case available for purchase if you want one — it keeps ours all together.

What to know before you buy: measure your camper door side first so you order enough panels to make a good-sized run. Taller dogs need the taller panels. And like any fence, do not leave your dog out there unsupervised. This is for hangout time with Mama and daddy, not babysitting.

Mama and daddy were extremely happy with the ease of assembly.
